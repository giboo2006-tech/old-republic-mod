# Saber assets: lightsaber, hilt parts, kyber crystal, workbench

All textures are **placeholders**: original art generated by `tools/assets/gen_textures_saber.py` (PIL, deterministic;
the script also writes every model, item definition, blockstate and particle JSON listed below, except, since
Milestone 7, the hilts, which `tools/assets/hilts/build.py` writes). No Star Wars game or film assets are used or
imitated. Since Milestone 7 the hilts are detailed, round models in the films' engineering language, but every design
is original and none copies a named film prop: the rules are in [Milestone 7: the hilts](#milestone-7-the-hilts).
Sounds are the shared placeholder sounds from `ModSounds` (`SABER_*`, `WORKBENCH_SLOT`, `WORKBENCH_ASSEMBLE`; see
`docs/AUDIO_BRIEF.md`); this feature adds no sound files.

Paths are under `src/main/resources/`.

**Final-sound brief, `saber.hum.singing`.** A loop laid over the normal saber hum of a saber built around the singing
crystal, so it must sit above the hum (which lives below 400 Hz) and never mask it. A high, clear crystal tone, as if a
wet finger circled a crystal glass: a fundamental near E6 with a fifth above (B6) and a faint octave, each beating slowly
(1 to 2 Hz) against a partner a hair off pitch, so it shimmers rather than whistles; a breath of air on top; a short
bright room tail. No melody and no attack: it is a layer, steady from the first sample, seamless at the loop point
(any length that loops cleanly, 2 to 6 s). Peak well below the hum (the code plays it at 0.45). It must not read as an
alarm or tinnitus: keep the top end soft, nothing sharp above 8 kHz. Subtitle (already in `lang-parts/sounds.json`): "Singing crystal hums".

The singing crystal's blade has no art of its own: the blade is the normal tinted blade, in `BladeColor.AMBER`
(`color.oldrepublic.blade.amber`) by default.

## Milestone 7: the hilts

Every hilt part was rebuilt as a detailed, round 3D model, and the inventory, the workbench's projector and the thrown
saber show the real hilt (`docs/MILESTONE_7.md`, sections 6 and 10). The hilts are code. Each part is a `Design` in
`tools/assets/hilts/` (`emitters.py`, `grips.py`, `pommels.py`), built with the model library `lib.py` (octagonal
prisms, lathes, rings, side details, turns, the Penitent's bend, the staff's two ends and a 64 × 64 sheet painter) and
the shared swatches of `common.py` (`textures/item/hilt/common.png`). `python3 tools/assets/hilts/build.py` writes
every hilt model, sheet, item definition and icon from them: `--only` runs some of its passes (`emitters`, `grips`,
`pommels`, `items`), `--out DIR` makes a dry run, `--strict` fails on a design's problems, `--prune` deletes the old
files no model names any more, and `--flat-icons` gives the inventory flat icons again. `preview.py` renders any
design, hilt, staff or material with the game's face, UV and rotation rules into a scratch folder, and `python3
tools/assets/hilts/lib.py --designs` checks every design against the rules below; CI runs it. Edit the designs, never
the generated files. The files are listed in `docs/ASSET_MANIFEST.md` (Milestone 7). Every design is a first version
made by a script and judged from previews; the `visuals` workflow's `hilts` pictures are the review.

### Art rules

These replace the old "generic cylinders" rule. `Design.problems()` checks the measurable ones: the bands, the joins,
the model bounds, the element count and the glow level.

- **The films' engineering language, original designs.** Machined metal tubes, black rubber grip ridges, activation
  boxes with a button on the thumb side, emitter shrouds with vents and a dark recessed mouth, bevelled pommel caps,
  D-rings and small brass accents. No design copies a named film prop, and no hilt carries a logo, text, glyph or
  rune.
- **The art direction holds** (`docs/DECISIONS.md`): no ground circles, sigils, halos or glowing runes, on a hilt or
  in its effects. Legendary parts have no spikes (`docs/assets/sabers_m5.md`): they read as old, careful craft.
- **Round, not square.** Bodies are regular octagonal prisms (`lib.octo`: two axis-aligned bars and two turned 45
  degrees); steps, rings, collars and grooves are octagons of other radii; buttons, boxes, ridges, vents and ports are
  small boxes. The Collector's squared parts are squared on purpose.
- **Colours.** A part's metal is a greyscale texture tinted by its material (`tintindex` 0, the tint source
  `oldrepublic:hilt_material`); black rubber and paint, dark steel, brass, leather, cord, lacquer, sunstone and glass
  keep their own colours. One coloured button per hilt at most: only the Guardian's (red) and the Duelist's (blue);
  the grips' switches are plain. The emitter's mouth is near black.
- **Glow.** Only a small glass window or inlay may glow, faintly (`light_emission` up to 6): the Reliquary's amber
  window, the Penitent's ember inlays or the Choir's violet glass. Nothing else glows.
- **Read at a glance.** Details must read in first person (the hilt about 120 pixels tall on an 854 × 480 screen) and
  on a stand-in at three blocks. Prefer a few strong forms over noise.
- **Conventions.** The hilt's axis is model x 8, z 8, along +y. The thumb side, which first person faces, is +x:
  activation boxes and buttons face +x. The Warden's quillons run along ±z, across the view. A part keeps to its band
  (pommel y -2.0 to -0.1, reaching -3.0 for rings and loops; grip -0.1 to 3.7; emitter 3.7 to 6.0, reaching 7.6 for
  tall shrouds; the staff grip 4.6 to 11.4), meets its neighbours at collars of radius 1.05 to 1.15 and has at most 60
  elements.
- **The staff is point-symmetric.** Its upper emitter is the design moved up 7.7; its lower one is the upper one
  turned 180 degrees about z through (8, 8, 8), never mirrored, so its faces stay outward and a bent emitter keeps its
  hand at both ends.

### What the builders learned

- **The mouth.** A lit blade's core hides about 1.05 units round the axis at the mouth, so mouths are about 1.0
  half-width and anything inside that vanishes while the blade burns; crown rims, fins, teeth and pipes stay in sight.
- **Octagon faces.** An octagon's bars have outward faces only, so an opening needs a niche behind it and a bend needs
  overlapping pieces, or the inside shows through.
- **Gaps.** A part standing off the body needs a gap of at least 0.1, or none; faces in one plane need at least 0.05
  between them, or they flicker.
- **First person.** Anything along +x points at the camera and is foreshortened: the Penitent's bend reads in third
  person, on a stand and in the inventory, not in first person, and side blades along ±x would hide, which is why the
  quillons run along ±z.
- **Small sizes.** A design must survive a 16-pixel slot: a strong outline and one clear pattern (ridges, wrap, rings,
  knurl) read; fine detail and dark materials (clan iron, duskglass) do not.

### Blades, icons and the throw

- **Blades.** Each emitter's item definition case gives its blade the design's mouth, the Penitent a tilt of 22.5
  degrees toward the thumb side, and the Warden two quillons: side blades a third as wide and 3.5 units long, lit and
  put out with the main blade, drawn but never hit-tested, with no trail of their own.
- **Inventory icons.** A saber or staff is its 3D hilt seen from the thumb side, on the old icon's diagonal (pommel
  lower left, emitter upper right), with a short blade stub while lit (the Warden's with its side stubs). A loose part
  stands upright (the staff grip lies diagonal). If the 3D icons read worse than flat ones, `build.py --flat-icons`
  brings back flat icons rendered from the designs, with an untinted `_detail` layer over the tinted metal.
- **The thrown saber** flies as itself: its own model at 0.68 of its held size (a staff at 0.62), spinning about the
  grip 20 times a second, with the sweep of light fed by where each blade is drawn.
